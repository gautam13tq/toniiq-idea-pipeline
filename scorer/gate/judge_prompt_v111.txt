BASE RULES (apply first; the owner's decisions below override them where they differ, and later owner rounds override earlier ones)
- B1 Hero must lead. IN if the hero ingredient is the product: it is the first active named in the title (ignoring brand, benefit words and N-in-1 counts), or the label leads with it, or it is the clearly dominant active. A hero-led formula where other actives are minor helpers stays IN (HERO_LED_COMPLEX). Another active named before the hero in the title, or another active that LEADS the label, makes it OUT (HERO_SECONDARY, R7-1) unless an exception below applies.
- B1a Exceptions to R7-1 (Round 13):
  * R13-6: the hero listed first on the label and named alone in the title counts even if a LATER label active has a higher dose. R7-1 bites only when another active LEADS the label (or is named before the hero in the title).
  * R13-1: vitamins and minerals listed first on a supplement-facts panel do not count as "leading" when the hero leads the title (hero + vitamins/minerals products count). This does not rescue a product where a different botanical or active leads.
  * R13-5 / R10-1: equal billing counts. A 2-in-1 title "X & [hero]" (exactly two named actives presented as a pair) counts even when X is named first and even without a label; an explicit "N-in-1" count with the hero co-equal counts. A longer list ("A, B & hero") or a hero-only title over a blend led by something else (e.g. "Triple Blend [hero]" whose label leads with another active) is still R7-1 OUT.
  * R13-2: a classical named formula whose name includes the hero (e.g. "[hero] Guggulu", Ayurvedic/TCM named formulas) counts even when another ingredient leads the label. Non-classical blends follow R7-1.
  * R13-4: carrier oils and fats (MCT oil, olive oil, lecithin, saturated fat rows) are not actives and are never compared against the hero. Likewise (NPD reading of R13-4) a food base or sweetener the product is delivered in (honey, sugar, syrup, pectin, gelatin) is a carrier, not a leading active. Vitamins, minerals and enzyme activity units are not compared by mg against the hero either.
  * Ties: when the hero and another active have the same dose, the hero leads if it is named first in the title.
- B1b R13-3: a derivative compound of the hero (dihydro-, tetrahydro-, hydroxy-, acetyl- ... + hero name, e.g. dihydroberberine) is its own shelf and is OUT on the parent hero shelf.
- B1c Names. A misspelling or closed-compound spelling of the hero (one or two letters off, missing spaces) still names the hero. A different word that only looks like the hero (a different fruit, a different compound such as L-carnosine on a zinc-carnosine shelf, a prebiotic on a probiotic shelf) does not.
- B2 Form. If the shelf requires a chemical form, the hero must be sold in that form (the label or title says that form). A different or unstated form is OUT, except where an owner decision counts it (e.g. a precursor pair that makes the form in water, R12-1).
- B3 Format. If the shelf requires a product format, the listing must be that format; a different or unstated format is OUT. Softgels count on a capsules shelf (R13-4). A resin, paste, gel or "cleanser" word does not by itself decide format or category: judge the product as a whole (an oral supplement sold as capsules/resin/paste with a serving is a supplement).
- B4 Not a competitor. OUT for: pet products, infant or kids-only products, topical/cosmetic products (salves, ointments, essential oils sold for aromatherapy/topical use, bath and skin products), seeds sold for planting, room/linen sprays, juice or beverage shots and other foods/teas/beverages/grocery or cooking ingredients (e.g. a cooking starch) not sold as dietary supplements, medicines/laxatives (drug facts), devices, books, and bundles of unrelated products. Judge by directions, serving and the listing as a whole, not the category or one word alone.
- B4a Bulk powders (R5-4 and the v2.3 "serving in the copy" note): a bulk single-herb powder, cut herb or dried herb sold by weight is OUT (EX_NOT_SUPPLEMENT) unless the listing presents a SUPPLEMENT serving: a supplement-facts serving size or servings per container, a stated mg/g amount per serving framed as a daily supplement dose, or capsules/tablets per day. Culinary or tea copy does NOT make it a supplement: "steep a teaspoon as tea", "add a teaspoon to smoothies, juice, recipes or baking", "use as a spice/food colouring" leave it OUT even though a teaspoon or scoop is mentioned. When in doubt about a plain powder pouch with only a scoop/teaspoon suggestion, it is OUT.
- B5 When the listing is thin (no label panel), judge from title, bullets and item details together; do not assume a hidden formula, and do not invent ingredients.
